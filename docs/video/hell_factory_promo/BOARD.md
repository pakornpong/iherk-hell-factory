# Hell Factory — the board

**One sentence.** "Your AI agents get an office you design yourself — the floor, the zones, every piece — and then they
show up and work in it."

**The product, and the claim.** Two real repositories that work as one. `iherk-hell-factory` is a live isometric
pixel-art office in the browser: every Claude Code, Gemini CLI, Codex and Antigravity session on the machine becomes an
employee who walks, sits, types and dozes as the session works. `iherk-pixcel` is the engine that draws every sprite in
it from small voxel models, in four directions. The viewer is a developer who runs coding agents all day. The one
claim: **the office is yours to design, freely** — resize and paint the floor, draw zones, place any of 7,500+ pieces —
and it is then where your agents really work. Every picture in the film is the product's own output: the office is the
maker's real 20 × 20 office, photographed from the running app in stages; the desk is drawn by pixcel from its model.

**What the film is held against.** No reference film was handed over, so the worked film it starts from, Plus One,
stands in (`vd new hell_factory_promo-anchor --like plusone`). Taken from it: one task done in order by a pointer, one
thing carried from the first beat to the mark, one move played many times with a count that climbs, the people arriving
as the peak, the opening question answered at the end. **What a viewer of a launch film expects, all kept:** the
trouble in their own terms first (agents at work with nowhere to be seen), the product doing its job in pieces of its
real picture, the mechanism (voxels → sprites), a proof with numbers that are counts of what is drawn, the name, what
it works with (the four tools), and the two repository names to start from.

**The world.**
- *Its line:* an office is a floor of tiles, and the film sets it the way a stage is set — one empty floor dressed
  piece by piece until the cast walks on.
- *Name, mark and claim as one idea:* "Hell Factory" is what the maker calls the place his agents work; the claim is
  that you build that place yourself, so the line under the name is **"Hell is what you make it."** The mark is the
  app's own header — a small light and the name in mono capitals — with the light redrawn as one floor tile: the film's
  first tile. It is grey until agents arrive and green after.
- *What this kind of film usually looks like, and what is left:* pixel-art trailers come in chunky pixel type, scanlines
  and chiptune from end to end. The subject stays; that treatment goes. Type is clean Geist and Geist Mono, the ground
  is soft moving colour, so the only pixelated thing on screen is the product. *Tension:* playful without being a toy,
  exact without going cold. The safe version (screen recording with captions) was left because it cannot show the
  office being made.
- *What everything is made of:* the real office in layers (floor tiles, walls, 210 furniture pieces in 36 diagonals,
  39 seated agents, three themes); the desk model in 11 real renders and four directions; 83 real pieces for the
  library wall. Colours: the app's sky blue `#38bdf8` is the hand (pointer, selection, zones being drawn); green
  `#22c55e` is a live agent and nothing else; cream type on dark. The ground answers "which room am I in": near-black
  before there is an office, slate under the office, indigo on pixcel's bench, the app's own day and night colours for
  the shifts.
- *The moves:* **one thing carries through** (the floor, from one tile to the mark); **the answered opening** ("Where
  do your AI agents work?" → "In the office you built."); **two rooms, one door** (the camera goes into one tile to
  reach pixcel's bench and comes back out with the desk); **hold the result back** (floor, zones and furniture before
  any agent); **a colour is a noun** (green).

**The one idea.** The floor. It first appears at beat 8 as one tile where the sessions fell to a point; it is spread,
painted, walled, zoned, furnished and staffed; at the end it draws back to that one tile, which is the light in the
mark. Once on screen it stays (the bench is inside one of its tiles).

**The cast.**
- the session lines: mono rows, a tool at work in each, with nowhere to be. Job: the trouble.
- the pointer: sky blue. Job: the designer's hand; whatever it does is something the product lets you do.
- the tile, the floor, the walls, the zones: the real office's own. Job: the place.
- the desk: pixcel's `desk-dev-dual`, written an operation at a time. Job: the mechanism, then the first piece placed.
- the pieces: 210, in 36 diagonals, back to front. Job: the proof of "freely" (server racks, nap pods, vending machines). The pointer places them.
- the agents: 39 seated sprites and the Boss. Job: the peak. Green belongs to them.
- Type: Geist 600 for the large words, Geist 500 for sentences, Geist Mono capitals for labels and counts.
  The Thai cut (`kit/lang.ts`) says the same lines in Thai, set in Kanit beside Geist; the product's own names stay.
  Its claim line is the maker's own words for the selling point: "ออกแบบได้อย่างอิสระ". Its line under the name:
  "นรกจะเป็นแบบไหน คุณออกแบบเอง".

**The clock.** 120 beats a minute, 30 frames a beat, 19 bars, 76 beats, 2280 frames, 38.0 s.

**The turn.** Beat 52. Before it the office is a made thing with nobody in it and the music is light (shaker, kick and
snap); after it the tile is green, people are in the chairs, keys are being typed and the drums go four on the floor.

**Gifts.** The mark's light is a floor tile and turns green only when agents arrive (52). The desk on the bench turns
on the four notes that later open the agents' tune (32–35). "Night shift" is the app's real night theme named as what it is
for agents who never go home (48–52).

**Framings.** Screen-flat for the sessions; very close on one tile; wide on the whole floor (floor, zones, build,
answer — never more than 7 s at a stretch, and pushing); the bench, flat and large; three close rooms; close on one
cubicle block for the first agents; the mark. None holds a third of the film.

**Rules of this film.** Things arrive falling a short way and decelerate over about 14 frames; only a landing knock is
instant. Sky blue is the hand, green is a live agent. Only the product is pixelated. Counts are counts of what is
drawn. Words sit top left, counts bottom left, never over the office. A word has landed 12 frames before anything
happens to it.

**Hardest shot.** The build, 36–43.5: 210 separate pictures landing in order on the real floor, registered to the
pixel, with the camera moving. Built and looked at first.

**Sound.** F major. Fmaj7, Dm7, B♭maj7, C7sus, a bar each, from beat 8. Shaker from the first tile, kick and snap from
the walls, four on the floor from the clock-in. Meanings: chirp = a session; knock = something set down for good
(tile, walls, desk, shifts, clock-in, answer); wooden pluck = a thing placed (tiles, zones, rows, agents — the tune);
square note = one voxel operation; key click = typing; whoosh = the camera going through a door. One silence of
harmony: bars 1–2, before there is a floor.
