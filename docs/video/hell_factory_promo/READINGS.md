# Readings

One round, on cut 3: two blind line-up readers from different models (Sonnet and Opus), each given this film beside the
worked film it started from (Plus One, exported untouched), and one fault-finder (Opus) given this film alone. Then one
more fault-finder on the fixed cut. Readers see sheets of stills; none can hear.

## Line-up reader 1 (Sonnet), cut 3

Ranked Plus One first, this film second. Called Plus One a freelance motion designer's (about 60 % sure) and this film
**a skilled hobbyist's** (about 55 %): "it reads as a product-UI screen recording with title cards over it. The ideas
are fine but there are no scene-to-scene transitions designed to connect." Strongest stretch: the build, 18–21.5 s,
"the room goes from empty to dense"; second, the zoom into the desks at 26–28 s. Weakest: the dive through the floor
at 12 s ("a dissolve through a flat colour field, dull but not broken") and the room tour at 22–23.5 s ("the camera
lurches into close-up views of rooms with tiny captions"). Also: the library holds nearly still for 0.65 s; captions on
dim plates read as cut off; counter, footer, badge and zone labels too small.

## Line-up reader 2 (Opus), cut 3

Ranked Plus One first, "but only just", and called both films a freelance motion designer's (this one about 45 %, "it
could be a skilled hobbyist"). "B is cleaner and more coherent. Its best transitions are better than anything in A":
the push through the floor tiles into the voxel stage was its strongest moment. Its weakest stretch, and the reason for
the ranking: **26–34 s. "'Then they clock in' is told almost entirely by a small counter. The agents are invisible at
this scale. From 31.0 to 34.0 s it is the same wide shot with only the caption changing."** "Its main claim, agents at
work, cannot be seen, and much of its text is too small to read." Also: half-lit words mid-reveal read as a mistake; an
empty caption plate before its words; the day shift "clashes with the rest of the film".

## Fault-finder 1 (Opus), cut 3

Told the film back correctly, start to end. Guess of who made it: a skilled hobbyist (about 65 %), "most likely the
tool's own developer, rendering the product's scenes programmatically". The three it would fix first:
1. the agents' value arrives late and tiny, and the opening log lines never visibly become a worker;
2. "Then they clock in" shows nobody clocking in: "the hero claim is shown at a scale where you cannot see the employees";
3. the sprite-engine section is a detour that names a second product.

Its other faults: only one layout is shown under "Any layout you want."; day and night change only the light; the zones
vanish before the build; "Nap pods." is framed on the vending machines and the three room shots share their content
(near-jump cuts); the cursor crosses half the frame in 3 frames at 7.2 s; a dark band under the walls as they drop; the
walls are never named; "1 TILES"; one tile reads as four; text too small (code listing, directions, tool list, tool
labels, repository line, opening log); five text systems and one reveal used sixteen times; the mark's grey-then-green
light is unexplained; holds at 3.5–4.0, 4.0–5.1, 8.0–9.0, 17.0–17.55 and 31–34 s; no link or command on the end card.

Not acted on: the agents all look alike and two sit at desk ends (they are the app's own sprites and seating); the zone
names (the office's real ones: there is no Zone 1); the mark and wordmark; no link on the end card.

## Left as it is, and why

- **The pixcel section stays.** The brief was to present both repositories, and the mechanism is what a launch film of
  this kind owes. One reader named it the strongest moment and one the detour.
- **"Any layout you want." over one layout.** There is one real office to photograph. The proof offered is how odd its
  rooms are. A second and third layout would be the stronger proof.
- **The day theme** was kept in cut 4 and cut in cut 5, after a third reader said the same. The app has three themes;
  the film now shows two.
- **No link on the end card.** The two repository names are there; whether they are public is the maker's call.

Both line-up readers placed this film below the worked film it was held against, one by a wide margin and one "only
just". By the skill's own rule that means it is not yet at that film's level in a reading of stills; the person's eye,
at speed and with the sound on, settles it.
